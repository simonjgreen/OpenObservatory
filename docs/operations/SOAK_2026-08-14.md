# 12-hour soak, starting 2026-08-14

A shorter validation run than the 72-hour acceptance soak, taken after ADR-060
and ADR-061 to answer one question: **do the three failures those ADRs address
stay fixed under a continuous unattended run?**

This is **not** the acceptance soak. `ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md` requires 72
continuous hours and `CLAUDE.md` forbids the word "complete" until that passes.
This run is evidence toward it, and a decision point on whether to commit three
days to the full one.

## What is being tested

The 2026-08-10→13 soak failed at **99.865%** continuity against a **≥ 99.9%**
criterion — 349.3 s lost of 259,200 s. The largest identified contributor was
the retention sweep: an unbounded 2.978 s query on the capture event loop every
300 s, which cost ~7 s of audio an hour and forced ~12 ALSA device restarts an
hour. One of those restarts wedged the microphone for 3 h 35 min on 08-14.

ADR-061 removed that cost; ADR-060 made a wedge survivable and made silence
report `critical` rather than `degraded`.

Station frozen on `main` at `cc87df5`. **No deploy may land until this run
ends** — `deploy.sh` restarts capture and voids it.

## Pass criteria

1. **Continuity ≥ 99.9%** over the run, and reported *with its margin* — the
   failed soak missed by 0.035 pp and a bare "passed" hides that.
2. **No wedge.** `stream_restarts` and `open_failures` both 0, and no stretch
   where `block_age_s` exceeded `capture_silence_critical_s` (30 s).
3. **`capture_gap` rows not arriving in pairs ~300 s apart.** That beat is the
   retention sweep's signature and its absence is the thing ADR-061 claims.
4. **Every sweep completes inside `retention_batch_budget_s`** (1.5 s), with
   `tiers_skipped` empty or a trailing suffix — never all three, which is the
   "never reached a tier" alarm.
5. **No statement aborts.** `interrupted_tier` should stay null. If one occurs,
   the deletion counters are trustworthy as of `cc87df5` but the abort itself
   wants explaining.

## What this run cannot settle

- The full-hour drift test and the 72-hour acceptance criteria. Twelve hours is
  longer than any clean window this project has recorded (22.2 min) but shorter
  than a mechanism with a daily period.
- The 2026-08-14 wedge's *device-level* cause, which is still unexplained: no
  USB event, autosuspend off, `hw_ptr` frozen at 768 after an
  `snd_pcm_prepare()`. ADR-060 makes it survivable, not impossible. If it
  recurs, `stream_restarts` will now count it — which is itself new information.
- Whether the reclaimed-but-present orphan direction ever occurs: nothing in the
  project detects it (see ADR-061's review notes).

## Monitoring

`binary_sensor.<station>_station_healthy` in Home Assistant is fed by the same
`_health_payload()` as `GET /api/v1/health` (`api/app.py:309`), and ADR-060 made
that go `critical` — HTTP 503 — after 30 s of silence. Before ADR-060 a station
deaf for hours reported `degraded` with HTTP 200, which is why the 08-14 outage
was found by eye on the indoor display rather than by an alert.

## Result

**Passed, overwhelmingly — and then kept running for another 50 hours.**

The 12-hour mark was reached at 2026-08-15 06:02 UTC with nothing to report.
Nothing needed deploying afterwards, so the freeze held by accident and the same
stream continued unbroken to **62.7 hours**:

| | Failed soak (08-10→13) | This run, at 62.7 h |
|---|---|---|
| `continuity_ratio` | 0.998652 | **0.999935** |
| audio lost | 349.3 s | **2.85 s** |
| ALSA overruns | 23,135 | 5 |
| `discontinuities` | 1,260 | 5 |
| `stream_restarts` | 0 | 0 |
| `capture_gap` rows | 22–24/hour | **3 in 62.7 h** (18.6 h and 5.4 h apart) |

The three gaps have no 300 s beat. Under the old regime those 62.7 hours would
have produced roughly 1,500 gap rows. ADR-060 and ADR-061 hold.

## The 72-hour run this became, and how it ended

Because the stream never restarted, this run *was* the acceptance soak by
2026-08-17, 9.25 hours from completing at 18:02 UTC and passing at 99.9935%.

**It did not complete. The Raspberry Pi restarted at 2026-08-17 09:07 UTC,
8.9 hours short.**

The cause is not established, and the evidence is that there is nothing in
software to find: memory flat at 16% with 6.5 GB available, load average 0.20,
no kernel error in the boot that died, no undervoltage or thermal record, no
OOM, and exactly one `LINUX RESTART` marker across eight days of retained
sysstat. `sar` samples every 10 minutes and the last one before the restart
(10:00:28 BST) is unremarkable; the 10:10 sample never happened. That pattern —
abrupt, no shutdown sequence, no load — is consistent with an external power
interruption.

**Nothing reported the restart.** Capture reopened within 25 seconds, health
returned `ok`, and every counter that would have shown the discontinuity is
process-scoped and reset with it. It was found two days later by running
`uptime`. ADR-065 fixes that: an unclean restart is now a health note saying in
so many words that a measurement in progress is void.

## What the two days after it exposed

Neither of these was caused by the restart; both were found because of it.

- **ADR-062** — retention had stopped reclaiming entirely. 351 of ~526 sweeps
  deleted nothing while the disk climbed 3.8 GB/hour, because every tier's
  candidate query re-walked the rows it had already reclaimed and could no
  longer finish inside its 1.5 s budget. Fixed; the first sweep after the fix
  reclaimed 886 MB and disk began falling.
- **ADR-063** — every UTC timestamp the station wrote for those 49 hours was
  **106 seconds early**. Capture anchored its frame-to-UTC mapping 1 m 45 s
  before NTP first synchronised after the boot, and `StreamClock` never
  revisits that anchor. Continuity was 0.999949 throughout and correctly so:
  continuity is a monotonic-clock property and was genuinely fine. **A soak
  that passes every criterion in `ACCEPTANCE_CRITERIA.md` can still be
  producing systematically wrong timestamps**, which is worth knowing before
  the next run is treated as proof of anything.

## Next run

Starts from the deploy of 2026-08-19 (`92d2613`). Any 72-hour attempt should
now check, at minimum:

- `oo_station_unclean_restart` is 0 for the whole window (ADR-065);
- `oo_capture_clock_reanchors_total` is 0, or the step size is recorded and the
  affected timestamps are understood (ADR-063);
- `retention_sweep_keeping_up` stays true and the disk trend is flat or falling
  (ADR-062, ADR-064).

None of those were checkable during this run, which is why it looked clean
while two of them were wrong.


## Outcome of the runs that followed

Attempt 3 (from the 2026-08-19 deploy) died 2026-08-22T05:48:20Z, 7h43m short,
**confirmed by the operator as a mains power cut** — the first established cause
for any of these restarts, and the reading this document could only guess at for
2026-08-17.

**Attempt 4 passed the continuity criterion on 2026-08-25**: 72.107 hours
restart-free, 99.9948% against ≥ 99.9%, 0.597 s lost of a 259.2 s budget. It
began as the reboot from attempt 3's power cut. All three checks this document
lists above under "Next run" were checkable and were checked. Full record:
[`SOAK_2026-08-22.md`](SOAK_2026-08-22.md).
